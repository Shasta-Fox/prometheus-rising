Learn it fast

The Protocol in Ten Minutes

A whole session in seven short steps. The idea on the left, what to do and say on the right. Read it once and you have the shape of the work.

Start with the agreements

Every session begins the same way. Read the agreements out loud. This is how people learn that passing is allowed and that nobody will be forced to explain themselves.

  • DoWelcome people and give a moment to settle.
  • Say"Passing is always allowed."
  • ReadThe circle agreements, aloud, every time.
  • OfferNicknames and camera-off as normal choices, never as exceptions.

Check in with one sentence

One sentence each: how am I arriving? A person who says "fine" has said enough.

  • Ask"In one sentence, how are you arriving?"
  • If someone passesThank them and move on. No follow-up, no pressure.
  • WitnessNotes who is quiet and what is said, without commenting.

Listen to a pain report

The most common failure is suspicion. Replace it with curiosity and a timeline. Methadone's two clocks differ, so pain can return while withdrawal is still covered.

  • AssumeThe person is telling you what their day is like.
  • Ask"Tell me about a typical day."
  • MapDoses, pain, sleep, and work on one timeline.
  • KnowWithdrawal cover lasts about 24 to 36 hours. Pain relief lasts about 6 to 8.
  • ScopeNever change a dose. Help the person prepare a question for their prescriber.

Hold a story, not a verdict

Peer mentors share from lived experience. A story is an offer, not a prescription. The witness listens for what is not said and says what they heard.

  • Offer"Here is what happened to me."
  • AvoidAdvice, diagnosis, and comparing whose story is harder.
  • AllowAnyone can step back at any time.
  • WitnessReflects what was heard and what stayed quiet.

When someone is harmed, slow down

Harm gets repaired, not hidden and not punished by reflex. Follow the steps in order. They may take days.

  • 1 PauseCheck that everyone is safe.
  • 2 HearListen to each person separately.
  • 3 Name the impactFacts and effects, not blame.
  • 4 Ask what is neededThe person harmed, then the person responsible.
  • 5 Agree on repairWritten, specific, and time-limited.
  • 6 Follow upCheck in after one week and one month.
  • 7 Escalate only for safetyA second steward reviews. Removal is explained and comes with referrals.

Know the crisis path by heart

Memorize this. Everything else can be looked up.

  • StayStay with the person.
  • AskAsk directly about suicide or danger.
  • Share988, call or text (US). 911 for a medical emergency.
  • OverdoseCall emergency services, give naloxone, and support breathing. See the cheat sheet.
  • TellLet a steward know, then check in afterward.

Close the circle

One small optional commitment each. Then close the same way every time, with the numbers in the room.

  • InviteOne small, optional step each person might take this week.
  • Say"Crisis numbers: 988, and SAMHSA 1-800-662-4357."
  • Say"What is said here stays here. What is learned here can leave."

If someone is in danger

Peer circles and this protocol are not emergency services. In the United States, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or call 911 for a medical emergency. SAMHSA's National Helpline is 1-800-662-4357 (free, confidential, 24/7). Carry naloxone where opioids are present and learn how to use it. Outside the US, use your local emergency number.
